When owners hesitate between geothermal (ground-to-water) and an air-source heat pump (air-to-water), their first reflex is often to compare the upfront installation price. For a property investment, however, the only relevant view is the **total cost of ownership (TCO)** over 25 years. At **WellDoneDrill**, we have analysed the figures. Here is why geothermal wins the economic match over time.

### 1. The initial investment: the borehole is an asset, not an expense

It is true that a geothermal heat pump costs more at the start, mainly because of the 150-metre borehole.

- **Air-to-water heat pump:** About €12,000 to €18,000.
- **Geothermal:** About €25,000 to €35,000 (depending on the borehole). But there is a major difference: the outdoor unit of an air-to-water heat pump is a machine that wears out, while the borehole drilled by WellDoneDrill is **property infrastructure** that lasts more than 50 years.

### 2. Electricity consumption: the weight of the SCOP

The efficiency of an air-to-water heat pump drops when it is cold outside (exactly when you need it most). Geothermal, by contrast, draws on a source at a constant 12 °C.

- **Air-to-water heat pump:** Seasonal COP (SCOP) of about 3 to 3.5.
- **Geothermal:** SCOP of about 4.5 to 5.5. Over 25 years, for an average house, geothermal uses around **30 % to 40 % less electricity**. With energy prices expected to rise, this saving adds up to tens of thousands of euros over two decades.

### 3. Lifespan and equipment replacement

This is where the difference becomes striking.

- **Air-to-water heat pump:** Exposed to the weather, frost and intensive defrost cycles, it lasts **12 to 15 years** on average. Over 25 years, you will have to replace it **twice**.
- **Geothermal:** The heat pump sits indoors, in a dry and stable environment. It works less hard because the source is constant. It lasts **20 to 25 years**.
- **The borehole:** It is never replaced. In 25 years, if you change the machine, you simply reconnect to your existing probes.

### 4. Maintenance and repair costs

An outdoor unit (air-to-water) needs regular fin cleaning, suffers from corrosion and has exposed moving parts (the fan). Geothermal is a sealed, passive circuit underground with a simple machine in the basement. Repair costs are statistically much lower for ground-source systems.

### 5. Geocooling: free air conditioning

In summer, an air-to-water heat pump has to reverse its cycle to cool, which uses a lot of electricity. Geothermal uses **passive geocooling** (only a small 50 W circulator runs). Over 25 summers, cooling comfort is almost free with geothermal, while it is expensive with an air-source system.

### 25-year balance (estimate for a 200 m² house)

- **Initial installation:** Air-to-water €15,000 · Geothermal (machine + borehole) €30,000
- **Replacement in year 13:** Air-to-water + €12,000 · Geothermal €0
- **Electricity (estimate):** Air-to-water €45,000 · Geothermal €28,000
- **Maintenance and repairs:** Air-to-water €4,000 · Geothermal €1,500
- **TOTAL OVER 25 YEARS:** Air-to-water (2 machines) €76,000 · Geothermal (1 machine + borehole) €59,500

Although the entry ticket is higher, geothermal delivers a net saving of more than **€15,000** over 25 years, while offering greater comfort (silence and geocooling). Choosing WellDoneDrill means choosing real profitability and added value for your property.
