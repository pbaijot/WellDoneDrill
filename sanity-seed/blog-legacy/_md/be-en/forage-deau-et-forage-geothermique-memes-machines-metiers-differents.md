In Belgium, the word "drilling" refers as much to water wells as to geothermal probes. From the outside, it looks like the same kind of site: a rig, pipes, a deep hole in the ground. Yet a water well and a geothermal borehole have neither the same purpose, nor the same constraints, nor the same sizing.

## Two types of borehole, two uses of the subsurface

A water well is meant to tap a resource: an aquifer. The aim is a flow rate, a quality and a reliable supply for a house, a farm, irrigation or a small industry. The well is fitted with casing, a screen and a submersible pump. The water comes up to the surface, flows through the distribution network and is then used or discharged.

A geothermal borehole works the other way round: nothing is "taken" from the subsurface; heat is exchanged with it. With vertical probes, the borehole is completely filled with an HDPE probe and geothermal grout. The fluid circulates through the probe in a closed loop, drawing heat from the ground or releasing it, but never bringing groundwater into direct contact with the heating circuit.

In one case, the subsurface is a water reservoir to be exploited.

In the other, it becomes a heat exchanger used to supply a heat pump.

### Two key terms to remember

- **Water well:** abstraction, flow rate, water quality, submersible pump.
- **Geothermal borehole:** probe, heat exchange, closed loop, heat pump.

## Water wells: looking for the aquifer

With a water well, the goal is to locate and exploit an aquifer:

- the well is sized according to the required flow rate;
- screens and filters are chosen according to grain size;
- a submersible pump is installed to bring the water up;
- water quality is checked according to its use (irrigation, process water, etc.).

Performance is measured in m³/h, in water-level stability and in water quality. Managing the resource (aquifer recharge, impact on neighbours, etc.) becomes an issue when flow rates are high.

## Geothermal probe boreholes: no groundwater required

With a geothermal probe borehole, the presence of an aquifer is not a requirement. What matters is the overall ability of the subsurface to transmit heat.

The borehole is fully occupied by:

- one or more HDPE probe loops;
- geothermal grout ensuring thermal contact with the ground.

The sizing concerns:

- the thermal output to be supplied;
- the total probe length (metres drilled);
- the spacing between probes;
- the seasonal balance between heat extracted and heat returned.

Groundwater, if present, remains isolated by the grout and any casing. The installation runs as a closed loop.

## Open-loop geothermal: when a water well becomes a heat source

There is one special case where water wells and geothermal meet: open-loop geothermal. Here, groundwater is used directly as the heat source for a heat pump.

The principle:

- an abstraction well draws water from the aquifer;
- the water passes through a heat exchanger or the heat pump, which recovers the heat;
- the water is then returned to the aquifer through an injection well.

This type of system is especially suited to large projects: big commercial buildings, heat networks, industrial sites and so on. It requires:

- an aquifer with sufficient flow;
- in-depth hydrogeological studies;
- a strict regulatory framework.

In this context, the water well truly becomes the "source" of the geothermal system. But it is no longer a simple well: it is a system designed to work as a loop while respecting the balance of the aquifer.
