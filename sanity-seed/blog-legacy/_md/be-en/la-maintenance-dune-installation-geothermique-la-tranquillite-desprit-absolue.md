One of the main obstacles when choosing a new heating system is the fear of breakdowns and the cost of annual servicing. Yet geothermal stands out radically from gas or oil boilers thanks to its mechanical simplicity. At **WellDoneDrill**, we like to say that a well-designed geothermal installation is a system you end up forgetting, it is so unobtrusive. Here is why geothermal needs the least maintenance on the market.

### 1. An investment for a century

The most impressive part of vertical geothermal lies beneath your feet. Once our teams have injected the geothermal grout and sealed the HDPE (high-density polyethylene) probes at 150 metres, the system is protected from any outside damage.

- **Zero maintenance:** The probes require no human intervention at all. They don't clog, don't corrode and never need cleaning.
- **Exceptional lifespan:** HDPE is an incredibly stable material. The lifespan of the probes is estimated at over 50, even 100 years. It is a property asset that will probably serve several generations of owners.

### 2. Inside the house: the reliability of a sealed circuit

Unlike a combustion boiler, a geothermal heat pump involves no flame, no soot and no flue gases. That changes everything for maintenance:

- **No chimney sweeping:** With no flue, this chore and the risks of carbon monoxide disappear.
- **A sealed circuit:** The heat-transfer fluid circulates in a closed loop between the borehole and the heat pump. A simple pressure check (once a year, like a car tyre) is usually enough to confirm it is working properly.
- **Accessible filters:** The only regular task is cleaning or replacing the dirt filters on the hydraulic circuit to keep the water flow optimal. It is a simple job any owner can learn in a few minutes.

### 3. Why is it simpler than an air-to-water heat pump?

Geothermal is often compared with air-to-water heat pumps. When it comes to maintenance, geothermal wins hands down:

- **No exposed outdoor unit:** An air-to-water heat pump has an outdoor fan exposed to frost, rain, dead leaves and dust.
- **No intensive defrost cycle:** In winter, an air-to-water heat pump has to stop to defrost its fins, which puts heavy strain on the compressor. With geothermal, the heat source (the ground at 12–13 °C) never freezes. The compressor works in stable conditions, which greatly reduces the risk of mechanical failure.

### 4. Professional follow-up: the inspection visit

Although the system is robust, an annual or two-yearly inspection by a specialist technician is still recommended. This quick check-up makes it possible to:

- Check the settings to optimise consumption.
- Test the quality of the heat-transfer fluid (antifreeze protection).
- Make sure the circulators are running at peak efficiency.
